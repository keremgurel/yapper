# Advanced edit benchmark

This benchmark compares Yapper's November 25, 2026 overlay workflow with a hand-built advanced edit and records the gaps observed in two runs.

[PR #377](https://github.com/keremgurel/yapper/pull/377) subsequently added partial rollback and refunds. With the updated Mac app, passing overlays stay on the timeline when another card fails its rendered check. If no card survives, eligible design and planning credits are refunded. The results below describe the original runs.

The reference is episode 15 of Kerem's founder update series, edited by hand in Remotion in the CELPIP repo. That edit is an internal benchmark only. Its numbers, its lines and its frames stay out of this repo: the beats below are described generically, and every frame lives on the machine that ran the test (see "Frames").

## Short answer

In the benchmark, Yapper covered few of the reference edit's beats. Asked the way a creator would ask today ("add overlays where they would improve the video"), Yapper picked one moment out of eleven, the review step rejected the card it designed, and the whole edit was rolled back. The video came out with captions and nothing else, and the account was still charged 40 credits.

Asked a second time with a long instruction that named each beat and the look, Yapper put four overlays on the timeline, each within about half a second of where the reference puts the same beat. Three more failed. The four that landed are small dark cards at the top of the frame. None of them uses the creator's own screenshots or recordings, none sits behind the speaker, none uses a split layout, and none has a sound.

When told what to do, the editorial pass found the right words to anchor on and chose sensible visual types. What it cannot do is reach the creator's own material, lay the frame out differently, or hold a consistent look across cards.

## What was run

Both runs went through the Mac editor's own "add overlays" flow (`EditorSession.performGeneratedOverlays`), the same code the Chirpy button runs: timeline inspection frames, face detection, the direct pass, the layout solver, design in batches of three, then the rendered-frame review with up to two repairs. The backend was `next dev` on 127.0.0.1 with fake auth and a throwaway local Postgres, calling the real providers.

The project was a clone (`cp -c`) of the `ep-15 fresh run` Yapper project: the raw 24 minute take after 1-Click Edit, cut to 2:04, with Yapper's own captions on. That is the state a creator would be in before asking for overlays. The reference edit uses a cut that is almost the same length, so beats were matched by the spoken words, not by timestamps.

|                              | Run 1                                                           | Run 2                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Instruction                  | The default, "Add overlays where they would improve the video." | About 600 characters naming seven beats and the card look (smoked glass, white text, red for cost, green for value) |
| Moments the direct pass kept | 1 of 5 it weighed                                               | 7 of 9 it weighed                                                                                                   |
| Designs that came back       | 1                                                               | 4 (2 failed layout checks, 1 failed in the model call)                                                              |
| On the timeline at the end   | 0 (review failed three times, everything rolled back)           | 4                                                                                                                   |
| Wall clock                   | 200 s                                                           | 364 s                                                                                                               |
| Credits charged              | 40                                                              | 130 (after partial refunds)                                                                                         |

Provider spend for both runs together was reserved at $4.52 across 35 model attempts. That is the list-price ceiling the spend guard reserves; what the gateway settles is usually lower and was not read separately.

To rerun it, start the same local server, then run the opt-in native test added with this doc:

```sh
cd native-macos
RUN_ADVANCED_EDIT_BENCHMARK=1 \
  ADVANCED_EDIT_OUTPUT=/abs/path/out \
  ADVANCED_EDIT_ENDPOINT=http://127.0.0.1:3141/api \
  ADVANCED_EDIT_PROJECT=/abs/path/clone.yapperproj \
  ADVANCED_EDIT_INSTRUCTION="optional" \
  swift test --filter AdvancedEditBenchmarkTests
```

It writes every request and reply, the edited project and a 1080 x 1920 export to the output folder, and never saves over the package. The local server needs `DATABASE_URL` and `DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED` pointed at a migrated scratch database with `user_local_test` seeded (credits and `subscription_status = 'active'`), `RATE_LIMIT_SUBJECT_SECRET` of 32 bytes or more, and `YAPPER_FAKE_AUTH=1`. Point it at a clone; the inspection frames in the requests are the creator's video.

Neither run asked anything before building. The storyboard opened with four questions: whether to cut a phrase the creator had said he never wants to say, which logo a named tool should use, where the real ad files are, and whether the spoken and on-screen series names should differ. Yapper has no step where it can ask these. It also cannot know the answers from the transcript alone.

Where run 2 placed its cards: all four at the top of the frame, between 62% and 68% of the frame width and 11% to 23% of its height. The reference's split visual takes 93% of the width and 40% of the height, and its full-frame cards are about the same size as Yapper's.

Captions are the closest match. Yapper's sit at the same height under the chin, in white heavy type with a soft shadow, two to four words a card. The differences are small: Yapper writes the series goal as a bare number where the reference writes it as money, keeps trailing commas, and does not move captions to the seam during a split.

The grade is visibly different. The reference applies the camera maker's log-to-Rec.709 LUT at 55%, which gives deeper blacks and warmer skin. Yapper's export of the same take is flatter and greyer, and the editor has no way to load a LUT.

Two things were not gaps. Timing was right: every card that landed in run 2 started within about half a second of the matching reference beat, and the review caught a reworded end line and fixed it. Count-ups also work: the scene language's `number` node did what the reference's counter does.

## What to do first

With partial rollback and refunds in place, gaps 2 to 7 are the smaller remaining changes. They would improve how many cards land and bring their sound and appearance closer to the reference. Matching the reference takes 9, 11 and 14 together: room to work (the split), components that build reliably, and the creator's own material inside them. Gap 10 (asking first) is what makes 14 possible, because the files the edit needs are not in the project until someone asks for them.

## Not checked

The exports were checked as stills at the matched beats, not watched or listened to. Only one take was tested, two runs in all, so the counts above are one sample each. The gateway's settled cost was not read. Production was not touched, and nothing was posted anywhere.
