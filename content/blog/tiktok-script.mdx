A TikTok script is the words you say, in order, plus a note on what appears on screen. Most work with four beats: a hook that opens the video, one point, proof or a demo of that point, and one ending. At a conversational 150 words per minute, a 30-second video is about 75 words.

The examples below are written for this guide. They are illustrative scripts, not transcripts of real videos, and the claims in them are placeholders you should replace with your own. If you want the long-form method for any video length, start with [how to write a video script](/blog/how-to-write-a-video-script). This page is the short-vertical version: shorter lines, a stronger first sentence and text that works with the sound off.

## The four beats of a TikTok script

1. **Hook (one sentence).** Name the problem, the result or the claim. Skip the greeting. "Hey guys, welcome back" spends your most-watched seconds on nothing.
2. **Point (one to three sentences).** Make the one point the video exists for. If you have two points, you probably have two videos.
3. **Proof or demo (one to three sentences).** Show the example, the number or the step. This is the beat viewers screenshot.
4. **Ending (one sentence).** One action: follow, save, try it tonight, or watch the next part. Do not stack three.

You will often hear a "three-second rule" for hooks. It is creator shorthand rather than a TikTok rule I can find in TikTok's own documentation. Treat it as an editing constraint: the first sentence carries the hook, so you should be able to say it in one breath.

## How many words is a TikTok script?

Divide your time by 60 and multiply by your speaking rate. The National Center for Voice and Speech puts average conversational English at about 150 words per minute ([NCVS](https://ncvs.org/tutorials/voice-qualities/)). If you pause for emphasis or cuts, 130 is safer.

| Video length | Words at 130 wpm | Words at 150 wpm |
| ------------ | ---------------: | ---------------: |
| 15 seconds   |               33 |               38 |
| 20 seconds   |               43 |               50 |
| 30 seconds   |               65 |               75 |
| 45 seconds   |               98 |              113 |
| 60 seconds   |              130 |              150 |
| 90 seconds   |              195 |              225 |

Platform limits are a ceiling, not a target. A one-point script is usually done in 20 to 45 seconds, and the five examples below run between 18 and 32 seconds. Read yours aloud once with a timer, then cut a line rather than speaking faster.

## Do you need a full script?

A common worry on creator forums is that a written script sounds robotic and an unwritten one rambles. The way out is to choose how much you write based on the video, not to pick one method for everything.

| Method         | Write                                                               | Best for                                       |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Hook only      | The first sentence, word for word. Three keywords for the rest.     | Opinions and stories you already know well     |
| Hook and close | The first and last sentence, word for word. Keywords in the middle. | Most talking-head videos                       |
| Full script    | Every sentence, read from a teleprompter or memorized.              | Steps, numbers, product claims, anything exact |

Whichever you pick, write the way you talk. A trick that works: say the point into a voice memo, type what you said, then tidy only the parts you tripped on. Written sentences are longer than spoken ones, and the quickest test is to read a line out loud. If you need a breath in the middle of it, split it. [How to memorize a script](/blog/how-to-memorize-a-script) covers the cases where you do want it in your head.

## Copy this TikTok script template

```text
BRIEF
[Viewer in a specific situation] will know / try [one thing].

HOOK (word for word, one breath)
[Problem, result or claim. No greeting.]
[on-screen text: the same idea in 6 words or fewer]

POINT
[The one thing you want them to take away.]

PROOF OR DEMO
[Example, number or step.]
[show: what is on screen during this line]

ENDING (word for word)
[One action.]

LENGTH CHECK
Spoken words: ___ ÷ my words per minute: ___ = ___ seconds
```

Put the on-screen text in the script. Viewers often scroll short video with the sound off, so the first line has to work as text as well as speech.

## 5 TikTok script examples you can adapt

Each table gives the spoken words per beat and the total. Times use 150 words per minute. Keep the structure, change the facts.

### 1. Problem and fix (81 words, about 32 seconds)

Use it when your viewer has a specific frustration and you have one fix.

| Beat    | Script                                                                                                     |  Words |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Hook    | If your script sounds robotic, the problem isn't the script. It's how you wrote it.                        |     15 |
| Problem | You wrote sentences for a reader: long ones, with commas and words you would never say out loud.           |     18 |
| Fix     | So say your point into a voice memo first. Then type what you said and tidy only the parts you tripped on. |     22 |
| Proof   | Read it back. If you need a breath in the middle of a line, split the line in two.                         |     19 |
| Ending  | Next video, start with the voice memo.                                                                     |      7 |
|         | **Total**                                                                                                  | **81** |

### 2. Myth and truth (54 words, about 22 seconds)

Use it when your field has a belief that sends people the wrong way. Make sure the "truth" is something you can back up.

| Beat   | Script                                                                              |  Words |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Hook   | You don't have to memorize your script to sound natural.                            |     10 |
| Myth   | People think natural means no notes.                                                |      6 |
| Truth  | Natural means you know your next point. That's all.                                 |      9 |
| How    | Learn the first line. Put three keywords on a card. Say the rest in your own words. |     17 |
| Ending | Record one take that way and compare it with your word-for-word take.               |     12 |
|        | **Total**                                                                           | **54** |

### 3. List of three (48 words, about 19 seconds)

Use it for checklists. Keep each item to one sentence so the video does not become three videos.

| Beat    | Script                                                                 |  Words |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Hook    | Three checks before you hit record, so you don't redo the whole video. |     13 |
| Point 1 | One. Record a ten-second test and listen back with headphones.         |     10 |
| Point 2 | Two. Put the light in front of your face, not behind you.              |     12 |
| Point 3 | Three. Say your first line out loud twice.                             |      8 |
| Ending  | Then record the whole thing.                                           |      5 |
|         | **Total**                                                              | **48** |

### 4. Opinion (57 words, about 23 seconds)

Use it to state a view and give the viewer a way to test it. Opinions need a reason, or they are just noise.

| Beat   | Script                                                                                                                   |  Words |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -----: |
| Hook   | Hot take: your first line matters more than your camera.                                                                 |     10 |
| Reason | A phone video with a strong opening can hold people. A sharp one that starts with “hey guys, so today” can get scrolled. |     23 |
| Test   | Cover the rest of your script and read only the first line. Would you keep watching?                                     |     16 |
| Ending | Rewrite that line before you touch the lights.                                                                           |      8 |
|        | **Total**                                                                                                                | **57** |

### 5. Quick tutorial (46 words, about 18 seconds)

Use it for a repeatable check or step list. Show each step on screen as you say it.

| Beat   | Script                                                                                  |  Words |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Hook   | Automatic captions can get names, numbers and “not” wrong. Check these before you post. |     14 |
| Step 1 | Names. Is every name and brand spelled the way it should be?                            |     12 |
| Step 2 | Numbers. Does fifteen say fifty?                                                        |      5 |
| Step 3 | Negatives. Did “can't” turn into “can”?                                                 |      6 |
| Ending | Scrub through once with the sound off. Then post.                                       |      9 |
|        | **Total**                                                                               | **46** |

The same shapes work for a story: replace the proof beat with a one-sentence "what happened" and a one-sentence "what I would do now". Write about something that actually happened to you, because a made-up story is easy to spot and not worth the trust it costs. ## Hook formulas to start from

You only need a small set of openers. Write three versions of the first line and pick the one you would not scroll past.

| Pattern  | Fill in                                            | Example                                                 |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Problem  | "If [situation], [what goes wrong]."               | "If your script sounds robotic, it's how you wrote it." |
| Result   | "How to [result] without [thing they hate]."       | "How to sound natural without memorizing anything."     |
| Mistake  | "Stop [common action]. Do [alternative]."          | "Stop writing in full sentences. Write what you'd say." |
| Number   | "[Number] checks before you [action]."             | "Three checks before you hit record."                   |
| Contrast | "Everyone says [belief]. Here's [what you found]." | "Everyone says memorize it. Try keywords instead."      |
| Question | "Why does [common annoyance] happen?"              | "Why do your captions get names wrong?"                 |

Whatever you pick, deliver what the hook promises by the ending. A hook that promises three fixes and delivers one leaves viewers feeling tricked.

## Use a TikTok script generator, then edit it

An AI script generator is useful for two things: getting a first draft when you are staring at a blank page and comparing several hooks. It is not good at facts, at your own experience or at sentences you can say in one breath, so the edit matters more than the draft.

TikTok has its own tool. A June 2023 Social Media Today report described a Script Generator in TikTok's Creative Center for advertisers: you enter an industry, product and keywords, choose a 15 to 30 or 31 to 60 second length, and it returns sample scripts with a hook, scenes and a call to action. TikTok's own disclaimer there says generated content may not be accurate and that you are responsible for what you publish. Availability can change, so check the current tool before relying on it.

Whichever generator you use, run the same checks:

- **Remove every claim you cannot back up.** AI drafts often invent statistics, results and customer quotes.
- **Read it aloud.** Split any sentence that needs a breath in the middle.
- **Cut the filler.** Delete "In today's video" and any greeting before the hook.
- **Put your own detail in.** One specific thing from your experience does more than three generic tips.
- **Match the length.** Count the spoken words and compare them with the table above.

## Recording and editing the script

Short videos reward a clean first line and a tidy edit. A few practical points:

- **Teleprompter or notes.** A narrow column of text near the lens keeps your eyes close to the camera. [What is a teleprompter?](/blog/what-is-a-teleprompter) covers size, scroll speed and a 20-second test take.
- **Record in beats.** If you stumble, restart the sentence, not the video. Leave a short pause before and after each beat so you can cut cleanly.
- **Frame for vertical.** [What is a talking head video?](/blog/talking-head-video) has a framing and lighting checklist.
- **Check the captions.** Read them with the sound off. Names, numbers and "not" are common places for automatic captions to slip.

## Common mistakes in TikTok scripts

- **Starting with a greeting or a setup.** Open with the problem or the result.
- **Two points in one video.** Split them.
- **Sentences written for the eye.** If you cannot say it in one breath, rewrite it.
- **A hook the video does not pay off.** Check the ending against the first line.
- **No on-screen text.** The first line should read as text too.
- **Copying a viral script word for word.** Borrow the structure, not the sentences, and never copy someone else's claims.

## Common questions

### How do you write a script for a TikTok video?

Write one sentence naming who the video is for and what they will take away. Then write four beats: a hook, one point, proof or a demo, and one ending. Say it aloud, split any line that needs a breath and check that the spoken word count fits your length. The template above is a fill-in version.

### How long should a TikTok script be?

A single-point script is usually 40 to 110 words, which is 15 to 45 seconds at a typical speaking pace. Use the word count table above: 75 words is about 30 seconds at 150 words per minute. TikTok sets maximum video lengths, but you do not need to fill them.

### What is the 3-second rule on TikTok?

It is creator shorthand for the idea that the opening seconds decide whether people keep watching. I could not find it stated as a rule in TikTok's own documentation. The useful version is to make your first sentence the hook and to be able to say it in one breath.

### Do I need to script my TikToks word for word?

Not always. A word-for-word script suits steps, numbers and product claims. For opinions and stories, write the first and last sentence and use three keywords in the middle. The mix that works best is the one that still sounds like you when you play it back.

### Can AI write a TikTok script for me?

It can draft one and suggest hooks, which is useful when you are stuck. You still need to check every claim, remove invented examples, read it aloud and put in your own experience. TikTok's own Script Generator says the same: you are responsible for what you publish.

### What is the best TikTok script generator?

It depends on what you need. TikTok's Creative Center tool is aimed at ads, general AI chat tools can draft anything but need good prompts, and Yapper's [AI script writer](/features/ai-script-writer) is built around editing a script you will read on a teleprompter. Judge any of them by how much you have to rewrite.

### Can I copy the script from a viral TikTok?

Borrow the structure: the hook pattern, the number of beats and the pacing. Do not copy the sentences or the claims. They were written for a different person, and they will sound off when you say them.
