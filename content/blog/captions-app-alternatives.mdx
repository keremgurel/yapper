## What Captions does, and what it costs

Captions describes itself as "AI that edits like a professional editor would". Its home page lists AI Edit styles that cut scenes and add B-roll, automatic captions with stylized fonts, translation and subtitles in 100+ languages, AI avatars and digital twins, eye-contact correction, denoise and trimming of pauses and filler words. It says you can use it on your phone or computer: sign up in a browser, or download the app from the App Store or Google Play.

Its pricing page lists a free tier, **Max at $24.99 a month** (500 credits, generative AI, AI actors and digital twins) and **Frontier from $69.99 a month** (1,400 credits and up). The page notes that features and prices reflect iOS plans.

People usually look for an alternative for one of four reasons: they want captions and simple edits without a subscription, they want a desktop editor, they mostly turn long videos into clips, or they want to record themselves rather than generate AI footage.

## Pick a Captions alternative by what you need

### Free captions and quick edits: CapCut

CapCut's home page offers to "try online for free" with no credit card, and lists auto captions, caption templates, text to speech and a video editor. Its pricing page starts with a free plan with templates and basic editing tools. Paid plans are shown in your local currency.

### Transcript editing on a computer: Descript

Descript edits video "as easy as using docs and slides". Its download page lists a desktop app for macOS 14 (Sonoma) or Windows 11 and newer. The free plan lets you try text-based editing and the AI tools; Hobbyist starts at $16 per person a month billed yearly.

### Clips from long videos: Submagic or OpusClip

Both are built around short-form clips. Submagic's Starter plan includes 15 captioned videos a month and 5 long videos clipped; OpusClip's free plan gives 60 credits a month with AI captions and auto reframe. Pick by how many clips you post each week.

### A teleprompter with captions: BIGVU

BIGVU pairs a teleprompter with captions and AI video tools, on the web and in an iPhone app. Plans start free. We compared it with other prompters in [best teleprompter apps](/blog/best-teleprompter-apps).

### Recording yourself, start to finish: Yapper

Captions can generate footage with AI avatars. Yapper is for videos where you are the one on camera. In one project you capture the idea, [write the script](/features/ai-script-writer), [record with a teleprompter](/features/teleprompter-recorder), [edit by transcript](/features/transcript-video-editor), [add captions](/features/automatic-captions) and post to connected YouTube, TikTok and Instagram accounts. Yapper runs on the web, with the desktop Studio app on Mac for recording and editing; mobile is coming soon. Writing, recording and editing by hand do not use credits. See [pricing](/pricing).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best alternative to Captions?

For free captions and editing, CapCut. For transcript-based editing on a computer, Descript. For clips from long videos, Submagic or OpusClip. For recording and publishing your own talking-head videos in one place, Yapper Studio.

### How much does Captions cost?

Captions is free to download with some features. Its pricing page lists Max at $24.99 a month and Frontier from $69.99 a month, and says the prices reflect iOS plans (checked November 25, 2026).

### Is there a free Captions alternative?

CapCut and Descript both have free plans, and OpusClip has a free plan with 60 credits a month. BIGVU also starts free.

### Does Captions work on a computer?

Yes. Captions says you can sign up in a browser on your computer, or download the app from the App Store or Google Play.

### Is Yapper only for Mac?

No. Yapper runs on the web for ideas, scripts and publishing, and the desktop Studio app on Mac handles recording and editing. Mobile is coming soon.
