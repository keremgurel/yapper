A good video script example is short enough to read in one sitting and shows its structure: a hook, one main point or offer, and a single next step. Below are six examples for the videos creators and small teams make most often: a promo, a product explainer, a how-to tutorial, a YouTube intro, a short vertical video and an internal update. Each one is broken into beats with a word count and a running time at about 150 words per minute.

All six scripts were written for this guide. They are illustrative, not transcripts of real videos, and the products in them are placeholders. Swap in your own facts and numbers before you use one.

## The six examples at a glance

| Example                 | Video type            | Words | Time at 150 wpm | Structure                                           |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- | ----: | --------------: | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Promo                | Marketing, paid ad    |    60 |         ~24 sec | Hook, offer, proof, ask                             |
| 2. Product explainer    | Website, landing page |   138 |         ~55 sec | Hook, problem, solution, how it works, proof, close |
| 3. Tutorial             | How-to, YouTube       |   144 |         ~58 sec | Result, what you need, steps, recap, close          |
| 4. YouTube intro        | First 30 seconds      |    74 |         ~30 sec | Hook, stakes, promise, bridge                       |
| 5. Short vertical video | Shorts, Reels         |    46 |         ~18 sec | Hook, point, demo, close                            |
| 6. Internal update      | Corporate, training   |   102 |         ~41 sec | Change, why, what to do, help, close                |

The time column uses the National Center for Voice and Speech figure of about 150 words per minute for conversational English ([NCVS](https://ncvs.org/tutorials/voice-qualities/)). Most people read a script a little slower, so time your own read once before you trim.

## 1. Promo video script example (marketing)

A promo has one job: get one kind of viewer to take one action. Lead with the problem they already feel, make one offer, back it with one concrete detail, and ask once.

**Brief:** People who have given up on language apps will start a free week of tutor feedback.

| Beat  | Script                                                                                   |  Words |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Hook  | Most people quit a language app in the first week. [App] sends you a voice note instead. |     17 |
| Offer | It pairs you with a real tutor who replies to your recordings within a day.              |     15 |
| Proof | You talk for one minute, they send back the three mistakes worth fixing first.           |     14 |
| Ask   | Your first week is free. Tap the link and record your first message tonight.             |     14 |
|       | **Total**                                                                                | **60** |

**Why it works:** The hook names a common experience in the viewer's own terms. The proof line is specific (one minute in, three mistakes back) instead of a claim like "the best way to learn". The ask names when to do it, which makes it easier to act on.

For a 15-second cut, keep the hook and the ask and fold the offer into one line. For a 60-second version, expand the proof line into a short on-screen demo rather than adding more claims.

## 2. Product explainer video script example

An explainer answers "what is this and why should I care" for someone who has never seen the product. The order that tends to hold attention is problem first, then the product, then how it works in plain steps.

**Brief:** Small finance teams that match payments by hand will connect a bank account and try automatic matching.

| Beat         | Script                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |   Words |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------: |
| Hook         | If your team still tracks invoices in a spreadsheet, someone is chasing a payment right now that was already paid.                                                                                                     |      20 |
| Problem      | Spreadsheets don't know when money arrives. So every Friday, someone opens the bank feed, the spreadsheet and their inbox, and matches them by hand.                                                                   |      24 |
| Solution     | [Product] connects to your bank and your invoicing tool and matches payments to invoices as they land.                                                                                                                 |      17 |
| How it works | When a payment comes in, [Product] finds the invoice with the same amount and customer, marks it paid and stops the reminder emails. If it isn't sure, it puts the payment in a short review list instead of guessing. |      39 |
| Proof        | You can see every match it made and undo any of them, so you are never trusting a black box.                                                                                                                           |      20 |
| Close        | Connect one bank account and see last month's payments matched in about ten minutes. The link is below.                                                                                                                |      18 |
|              | **Total**                                                                                                                                                                                                              | **138** |

**Why it works:** The problem section describes a task the viewer has done, down to the day of the week, so they recognize it before you mention the product. The "how it works" beat explains one real behavior, including what happens when the product is unsure. That line answers the objection a careful buyer would raise.

On screen, show the product during "How it works" and "Proof", and keep the hook on your face or on a simple text card. If you only have budget for one visual sequence, spend it there.

## 3. Tutorial video script example

Tutorials get watched by people who want to finish a task, so show or state the result first, list what they need, then number the steps. One step per sentence or two keeps the script easy to follow and easy to cut.

**Brief:** A beginner will record clean voice audio on their phone without buying a microphone.

| Beat          | Script                                                                                                                   |   Words |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------: |
| Result first  | By the end of this video, your phone will record clean audio without a microphone. It takes about two minutes.           |      20 |
| What you need | You need your phone, a quiet room and any soft surface, like a bed or a sofa.                                            |      17 |
| Step 1        | Turn on airplane mode, so a notification sound can't land in the middle of your take.                                    |      16 |
| Step 2        | Open the voice memo app, not the camera app, if you only need audio. It usually records at a higher quality.             |      21 |
| Step 3        | Hold the phone about a hand's width from your mouth, slightly to the side, so your breath doesn't hit the mic.           |      21 |
| Step 4        | Record ten seconds, then play it back on headphones. If it sounds hollow, move closer to the soft surface and try again. |      22 |
| Recap         | Airplane mode, voice memo app, a hand's width away, test on headphones.                                                  |      12 |
| Close         | Next video, I'll show you how to cut out the pauses in under a minute.                                                   |      15 |
|               | **Total**                                                                                                                | **144** |

**Stage notes** (what to show while you say each beat):

- **Result first:** play five seconds of the finished clean recording.
- **Step 1:** screen recording of the airplane mode toggle.
- **Step 3:** a side shot of the phone at the right distance, with a text label.
- **Step 4:** the playback on headphones, with the before and after audio.
- **Recap:** the four steps as a text list on screen.

**Why it works:** The viewer knows what they will get and how long it takes in the first sentence. Every step is an action, and the recap fits in one breath. If you write tutorials as two columns, put the script in one column and these stage notes in the other.

## 4. YouTube video script example (the first 30 seconds)

On YouTube, the opening decides whether the rest of the script gets heard. Write the intro word for word even if you use talking points after it.

**Brief:** Creators who edit weekly will compare timeline editing with transcript editing.

| Beat    | Script                                                                                                                                                               |  Words |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Hook    | I edited the same ten-minute video twice: once by scrubbing the timeline, once by deleting words from the transcript. One took fifty minutes. The other took twelve. |     27 |
| Stakes  | If you post every week, that difference is most of an evening.                                                                                                       |     12 |
| Promise | I'll show you both edits side by side, where the transcript method breaks, and the one setting that made the biggest difference.                                     |     22 |
| Bridge  | Let's start with the timeline edit, because that's how most of us learned.                                                                                           |     13 |
|         | **Total**                                                                                                                                                            | **74** |

**Why it works:** The hook makes a concrete comparison with numbers the video will show. The stakes line translates the numbers into the viewer's week. The promise lists exactly what is coming, including a limitation, which tells the viewer this is not a one-sided pitch. The bridge moves straight into the first section with no "before we start" detour.

Only use numbers you measured yourself. If you haven't timed both edits, change the hook to the question you are testing, for example: "Is editing from a transcript actually faster? I edited the same video both ways to find out."

## 5. Short vertical video script example (Shorts or Reels)

Short vertical videos are often watched with the sound off at first, so the hook should work as on-screen text too. Make one point, show it, and stop.

**Brief:** New creators will replace filler words with a pause.

| Beat                        | Script                                                                                               |  Words |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Hook (on screen and spoken) | Stop saying 'um' on camera. Try this instead.                                                        |      8 |
| Point                       | When you lose your place, close your mouth and breathe. Silence looks confident. Filler words don't. |     16 |
| Demo                        | Watch. Same sentence. First with filler, then with a pause.                                          |     10 |
| Close                       | Practice it once today on a ten-second take. Follow for part two.                                    |     12 |
|                             | **Total**                                                                                            | **46** |

**Why it works:** The first line is a complete idea in eight words, readable as a caption. The demo beat shows the change instead of describing it. At under 20 seconds, it leaves room for the viewer to watch twice, which a 60-second version of the same idea would not.

YouTube Shorts can run up to three minutes ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)), but that is a maximum. Most one-point scripts are done in 20 to 45 seconds.

## 6. Corporate or training video script example

An internal update or short training video needs to answer four questions quickly: what is changing, why, what do I need to do, and where do I get help. Put the change in the first sentence.

**Brief:** Employees will submit expenses in the new portal from Monday.

| Beat                | Script                                                                                                                                     |   Words |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------: |
| Hook                | Starting Monday, expense reports go through the new portal, and paper receipts are no longer accepted.                                     |      16 |
| Why                 | The old process took eleven days on average to pay you back. The goal of the new one is five.                                              |      20 |
| What changes        | Take a photo of each receipt in the app the day you spend it. Pick the project code from the list. Submit by the last Friday of the month. |      29 |
| What stays the same | Spending limits and approval rules have not changed.                                                                                       |       8 |
| Where to get help   | If something doesn't work, message the finance help channel. Someone will answer the same day.                                             |      15 |
| Close               | The full guide is linked below. Thanks for trying it in the first week.                                                                    |      14 |
|                     | **Total**                                                                                                                                  | **102** |

**Why it works:** Nobody has to wait to learn what is changing. "What stays the same" is a short line that heads off the most common worry. The steps are in the order the employee will do them. Keep numbers like "eleven days" only if they are real for your company.

## How to adapt any of these examples

1. **Write your own one-sentence brief first.** Use the pattern above: who the viewer is, and what they will do or know after watching.
2. **Keep the beats, replace every fact.** The structure transfers. The product names, numbers and claims must be yours.
3. **Read it aloud and time it.** Mark any line you stumble on and rewrite it the way you would say it.
4. **Cut to length by removing a beat, not by talking faster.** A promo without the proof line is weaker than one read at speed.
5. **Format it for reading on camera:** short lines, a blank line between beats and stage notes in brackets.

## Common mistakes in example-based scripts

- **Copying the claims along with the structure.** If an example says "within a day", that is a placeholder, not a promise you can make.
- **Two asks at the end.** "Subscribe, comment and check the link" splits attention. Pick the one action that matters for this video.
- **A greeting before the hook.** "Hey everyone, welcome back" costs three to four seconds of the most-watched part of the video.
- **Lines that read well but sound stiff.** Written sentences with several clauses are hard to say. If you need a breath in the middle of a line, split it.
- **No visual plan.** Even a talking-head video benefits from one note on what to show during the proof or demo beat.

## Common questions

### What is an example of a video script?

A video script is the words you will say, in order, with notes on what to show. The tutorial example above is a typical one: it opens with the result, lists what you need, numbers the steps, recaps in one line and ends with one next step. It runs 144 words, or about a minute at 150 words per minute.

### How do you write a script for a marketing video?

Write a one-sentence brief naming the viewer and the action you want. Then use four beats: a hook that names the viewer's problem, one offer, one specific piece of proof and one ask. The promo example above is 60 words, which is about 25 seconds.

### How many words is a 1-minute video script?

About 150 words at a conversational pace, or 120 if you read slowly with pauses. Time yourself once and use your own number. The explainer and tutorial examples here are each just under a minute.

### What is the difference between an explainer script and a promo script?

An explainer teaches someone what the product does and how it works, so it spends most of its words on the problem and the how-it-works beat. A promo assumes the viewer only needs a reason to act now, so it is shorter and built around one offer and one ask.

### Should a video script include visuals?

Yes, at least as notes. A two-column format, with the spoken words on one side and what appears on screen on the other, works well for tutorials and explainers. For a talking-head video, a few bracketed notes such as "[show the app screen]" are usually enough.

### Can I use AI to write a video script from an example?

You can give an AI tool one of these examples as a structure and ask for a draft about your topic. Tell it not to invent results, quotes or customer stories, then check every fact and read the draft aloud. AI drafts often have long sentences that are hard to say on camera.
