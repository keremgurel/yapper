To generate content ideas, collect a real question, connect it to something you have experienced, and choose one point you can demonstrate. You do not need a completely new subject every time. You need a specific reason for your audience to care about this version of it.

Here is a repeatable process for Reels, Shorts, TikTok or a longer talking-head video. You can do it in a notebook without making an AI call.

## Start with evidence instead of a blank prompt

Make three short lists:

- **Questions:** What did someone ask you in a comment, message, meeting or conversation?
- **Experience:** What did you try, change your mind about, get wrong or finally understand?
- **Material:** What can you show: a draft, an example, a before-and-after, a recording or a decision?

Use your own examples or material you have permission to share. Remove private details before putting a customer conversation into an AI tool or a public video.

A topic such as “productivity” is too broad to tell you what to record. “Why I stopped writing a script for every sentence” contains a decision, a person and a potential lesson.

## Turn one experience into five distinct angles

Suppose you make educational videos. You tried replacing a full script with three talking points. These are illustrative angles, not claims about a real creator’s results:

| Angle           | Content idea                                          | What to show                           |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| A useful change | How I prepare three talking points before recording   | The three-point outline                |
| A mistake       | Why my first teleprompter script sounded stiff        | A sentence before and after rewriting  |
| A comparison    | Full script or talking points: which fits this video? | Two versions of the same opening       |
| A demonstration | Turning one audience question into a short video      | The question and resulting outline     |
| A boundary      | When I still write every word before recording        | An example where exact wording matters |

These can become separate videos if each teaches something different. Changing the opening sentence of an already-published video is not enough to make a useful new idea.

## Choose the idea you can actually make

Before drafting, answer four questions:

1. **Who needs this?** Name a situation, not just a demographic: “a creator who loses their place while recording.”
2. **What is the one takeaway?** Write the change the viewer can make after watching.
3. **What makes it yours?** Identify the example or point of view you can contribute.
4. **Have you already covered it?** Check your published videos and current drafts. If yes, find a genuinely new example, answer a follow-up question, or choose another idea.

Keep ideas that pass all four. Save the others as notes; you do not have to turn every thought into a post.

## Use a content ideas generator with useful context

If you want AI help, give it the inputs you just collected. “Give me viral ideas” leaves it guessing about your audience, experience and voice. A more useful prompt is:

> I make videos for [audience in a specific situation]. They keep asking [question]. My experience is [what actually happened], and I can show [example]. My point of view is [what I believe and why]. I have already posted about [topics or links]. Suggest five distinct video angles, each with one takeaway, a possible opening and the evidence I should show. Flag anything that repeats my existing work. Do not invent results or personal stories.

Treat the output as a draft. Check that each idea fits what you actually know and have permission to discuss. A generator can suggest a fresh angle; it cannot establish that an invented story happened to you.

## Give the selected idea a recordable structure

A simple outline is enough to start:

- **Opening:** Name the viewer’s problem or the change you made.
- **Example:** Show one concrete case.
- **Takeaway:** Explain what to try and when it applies.

For the talking-points example, that could be: “I kept losing my place while recording. Here is the three-line outline I tried instead. Use it when you know the material; keep a fuller script when the wording needs to be precise.”

Read it aloud once. Shorten any sentence you would not say in conversation. ## Keep an idea bank that does not become another backlog

Separate raw notes from videos you intend to make. For each selected idea, keep its source, audience, takeaway and current status together. Before starting a new batch, check what is already ready to record.

## Common questions

### How do I come up with content ideas consistently?

Keep a small capture habit: save a question when it comes up and a lesson when it is fresh. Review those notes before your next recording session. A short list of specific, usable ideas is more helpful than a large list of generic topics.

### Can I reuse an old content idea?

Yes, when there is a meaningful new contribution: updated evidence, a better demonstration, a different audience question or a change in your view. Check the original post so you can say what is new instead of accidentally repeating it.

### Do I need AI to generate content ideas?

No. The questions, experience and material worksheet above works on its own. Use AI when comparing angles or structuring a thought would help you; you do not need to generate suggestions every time you open your workspace.
